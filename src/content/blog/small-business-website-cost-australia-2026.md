A professional website for an Australian small business usually costs between A$2,000 and A$10,000 to build in 2026, plus a monthly amount for hosting, security and updates. A simple five to eight page brochure site sits at the lower end. Online bookings, payments, integrations with your CRM and custom design push the price up.

The trouble is that two quotes for "a website" can describe very different things. This guide explains what the ranges cover, what drives the cost, and how to compare quotes fairly.

## What changes the price of a website?

Five things move a quote more than anything else:

1. **Number of pages and how much writing you need.** Copywriting is often the hidden cost. If you supply the words, the price drops.
2. **Custom design or a proven layout.** A layout built on tested components is faster and cheaper than a fully bespoke design.
3. **Bookings, payments and forms.** Online booking, deposits and quote forms need setup and testing.
4. **Integrations.** Connecting enquiries to your CRM, calendar, accounting tool or email marketing adds work, but saves far more in admin later.
5. **Search and speed work.** Page titles, descriptions, structured data, image optimisation and Google Business Profile setup are what get the site found. A cheaper quote often leaves them out.

## Is a cheaper website a false saving?

Sometimes. A low build price can be fine if you own the site and accounts, and the site is fast and secure. It becomes expensive when the domain is registered in someone else's name, nobody updates the plugins, or the person who built it cannot be reached. Before you choose on price, read [who owns your website, domain and data](/blog/who-owns-your-website-domain-and-data).

Ask every provider the same questions:

- Who will own the domain, the hosting account and the website files?
- What exactly is included: pages, copywriting, images, forms, bookings and search setup?
- What will it cost each month after launch, and what does that cover?
- Who updates and backs up the site, and how often?
- How do enquiries reach me, and can they go straight into a CRM?
- What happens if I want to move the site to another provider?

## How long does a small business website take?

A simple brochure site usually takes two to four weeks once content is ready. Sites with bookings, payments or integrations take longer. The biggest delay is almost always content: page copy, photos and approvals.
