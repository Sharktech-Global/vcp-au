If an agency builds your website, the agency usually owns the copyright in the code and design unless your contract assigns it to you in writing. The .com.au domain belongs to whoever is recorded as the registrant, which should be your business. Your Google Business Profile and customer data should sit in accounts you control, with the agency invited in.

Most ownership problems only surface when something goes wrong: the agency closes, the freelancer moves on, or you want to switch providers and find you cannot take your site with you.

## Do I own the copyright in a website I paid someone to build?

Not automatically. Under the [Copyright Act 1968, section 35](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html), the author of a work owns the copyright, and where an employee creates it under a contract of service, the employer owns it. For a commissioned website, that usually means the agency or the freelancer holds the copyright, even though you paid the invoice.

To move ownership to your business, the copyright has to be assigned. [Section 196(3)](https://www6.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s196.html) says an assignment has no effect unless it is in writing and signed by or on behalf of the person assigning it. A paid invoice or an email saying "it's yours" is not enough.

Two further points. Themes, plugins, fonts and stock photos are licensed, not owned, and no agency can assign what it does not own. And a platform website, built inside a subscription tool, is different from a custom build: the question becomes whether you can export your content and data, not whether you own the platform code.

This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm your position with your lawyer before you sign or end an agreement.

## Who owns the .com.au domain if the agency registered it?

A .com.au domain is a licence, not property. Under the [.au Domain Administration Rules: Licensing](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/au-rules-and-policies/au-domain-administration-rules-licensing-2/), the registrant is the person issued the licence and recorded as registrant in the registry. Applicants need an Australian presence, and for .com.au they must be a commercial entity whose name relates to their business name, trade mark or services.

An agent, such as a web agency, may apply for you, but must ensure your business is recorded as the registrant. The [.au Domain Administration (auDA) guidance on .com.au names](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/the-different-au-domain-names/com-au-domain-names/) warns that if a developer uses their own details, you may face eligibility issues and could lose control of the name if they stop trading or fail to pass on renewal notices.

So your business entity, using your Australian Business Number, should be the registrant. The agency can be a technical contact.

## What if my old freelancer registered everything in their own name?

This is common for service businesses in Coffs Harbour, Lismore and across regional New South Wales, where a local freelancer built the site years ago and has since moved away or changed careers. It happens in Sydney and Melbourne too, often when a friend "did the website".

Look up your domain on the .au WHOIS tool on the auDA website. If the freelancer is the registrant, they must start a change of registrant, because under the [auDA transfer guidance](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/manage-your-au-domain-name/transfer-your-au-domain-name/) only the current registrant can initiate it. Moving to a new registrar needs an authorisation code, which is sent to the registrant contact email. If that email is a dead address, solve it before the renewal date. If the freelancer cannot be reached, speak to the domain provider and auDA, and get advice before the licence lapses.

## What should I hold in my own name?

Anything in the middle column that sits with someone else is a risk.

| Item | Who should hold it | Agency's role |
|---|---|---|
| .com.au domain licence | Your business, as registrant | Technical contact or agent |
| Domain provider account | Your business email and login | Delegated access |
| Hosting account | Your business, or a written right to a full copy of the site | Administrator access |
| Website copyright (custom build) | Your business, by signed written assignment | Licence back for its portfolio |
| Google Business Profile | Your business, as primary owner | Manager |
| Analytics and search reporting accounts | Your business account | User access |
| Customer relationship management (CRM) data | Your business, with export rights in the contract | Works under your instructions |

## Who controls my Google Business Profile?

Google's [owners and managers help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) explains that a profile can have multiple owners but only one primary owner. Owners can add and remove users. Managers handle day-to-day work but cannot add or remove users or remove the profile.

Your business should be primary owner. An agency managing reviews and posts only needs manager access. If your agency is primary owner, ask it to transfer that role to you while the relationship is friendly.

## Can I take my customer data with me if I leave?

Your customer list, bookings and enquiry history are business assets. Your contract should say you can export all customer, booking and conversation data in a standard spreadsheet format at any time, including after you give notice, and that the provider deletes its copies on request once the export is done.

If your turnover is over the Privacy Act threshold, or you provide health services, you also have legal obligations for that personal information. The [Office of the Australian Information Commissioner small business guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-for-organisations/small-business) explains when the Act applies.

## What should the website contract say?

- **Copyright assignment:** on final payment, the agency assigns copyright in the custom code, design and content to you, in a signed document.
- **Third-party licences:** a list of themes, plugins, fonts and images, and who holds each licence.
- **Domain:** your business is recorded as registrant, with your contact details.
- **Access:** administrator logins for hosting, the content management system and analytics are handed over at launch.
- **Exit:** a full copy of the site files and database, plus a data export, within an agreed number of days.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does paying for a website mean I own it?

Not by default. The author or their employer owns copyright, and an assignment only takes effect if it is in writing and signed. Ask for a signed assignment in your contract.

### Can my web agency be the registrant of my .com.au domain?

It should not be. The auDA rules let an agent apply for you, but the agent must ensure your business is recorded as the registrant.

### How do I check who holds my domain?

Search your domain on the .au WHOIS tool on the auDA website. It shows the registrant name. If it is not your business, arrange a change of registrant before your next renewal.

### What access should an agency have to my Google Business Profile?

Manager access is usually enough. Managers can update information and reply to reviews, but cannot add or remove users.

### What happens to my website if the agency closes?

If you hold the domain, hosting access and a current copy of the site files, another provider can usually take over quickly. If the agency held everything, recovery can be slow and costly.
