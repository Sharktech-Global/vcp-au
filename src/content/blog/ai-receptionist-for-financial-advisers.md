An AI receptionist for financial advisers answers every call, day or night, in your practice's name. It handles general questions, books first meetings and annual reviews into your calendar, and passes urgent or complex calls to your team with a summary. It never gives financial advice. The best ones also follow up after the call.

That last part matters more than most buyers expect.

## Why do advice practices miss so many calls?

Advisers spend most of the day in meetings, preparing advice or on review calls. Reception is often one person, or nobody. Prospects ring at lunchtime or in the evening, hear voicemail and move on. Existing clients wait for a callback. Every missed call is either a lost first meeting or a frustrated client.

There are about 15,050 advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register, according to [Padua Wealth Data figures reported by Riskinfo on 17 July 2026](https://riskinfo.com.au/news/2026/07/17/adviser-numbers-top-15050/). Many work in small practices. That means a lot of talented people who cannot answer the phone while they are doing their real job.

## What should an AI receptionist do for a financial adviser?

It should answer every call within a few rings, explain what your practice does, book the right type of meeting, and send each caller a confirmation. It should log every call against a contact record, hand urgent calls to a person, and start follow-up for prospects who are not ready to book yet.

In practice, look for these jobs:

- **Answer and qualify.** Capture the caller's name, contact details and the reason for the call.
- **Book.** Put first meetings, reviews and callbacks straight into the adviser's calendar.
- **Route.** Transfer existing clients with urgent matters to the right person, with a short brief.
- **Record.** Save a summary of every call in your CRM, not in a separate inbox.
- **Follow up.** Send reminders before meetings and nurture messages you have approved.

If a tool only takes messages, it is a more polite voicemail.

## Can an AI receptionist give financial advice?

No, and it must not try. A well set up AI receptionist answers general questions about your practice, such as fees for a first meeting, office hours and what to bring. Anything about a person's own money, products or strategy is captured and booked with a licensed adviser. Your licensee obligations stay with your practice.

Write this boundary into the setup. Give the AI a short list of topics it can discuss, and a clear rule for everything else: take the details, book the meeting, tell the caller an adviser will help.

## What privacy rules apply to an AI receptionist?

Your practice stays responsible for the personal information the AI collects. The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) expects businesses to do due diligence on AI products, tell people when they are dealing with AI, update privacy policies, and keep humans able to check and correct AI outputs.

That is set out in the [OAIC guidance on privacy and the use of commercially available AI products](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products) (published 21 October 2024, updated 17 January 2025).

There is also a new rule coming. From 10 December 2026, if a computer program makes, or does a step substantially related to, a decision that could significantly affect someone's rights or interests using their personal information, your privacy policy must describe it. The OAIC published [fact sheets and a flowchart on this obligation on 30 September 2026](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/new-resources-on-transparency-for-use-of-ai-and-automated-decision-making).

An AI receptionist that answers questions and books meetings is unlikely to be making that kind of decision. But check with your licensee and your privacy adviser, and ask any vendor these questions:

1. Where are call recordings and transcripts stored?
2. Who can access them, and for how long are they kept?
3. Does the AI tell callers they are speaking with an AI?
4. Can you turn off recording or limit what is collected?
5. Can staff review and correct what the AI recorded?

## How do the options compare?

| Option | What you get | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Voicemail and callbacks | No cost | Most prospects do not leave a message |
| Human answering service | A person takes messages | Messages rather than bookings, and queues at busy times |
| Standalone AI receptionist app | 24 hour answering and booking | Often separate from your CRM and follow-up, support hours vary |
| In-house receptionist | Personal service in business hours | Wages, leave and no after-hours cover |
| VCPility AI voice agent | Answering, booking, CRM record and follow-up in one system | Built for enquiries and bookings, not for advice or compliance |

The real question is not who answers the phone. It is what happens next. A booked first meeting that has no reminder, and a prospect who is not ready yet with no follow-up, are both lost revenue.

## What happens after the call?

The call is only the start. A prospect who books should get a confirmation, a reminder and a short note on what to bring. A prospect who is not ready should get helpful, approved messages over the following weeks. An existing client should see their request logged and actioned. Without this, the AI just moves the problem.

Advice is a long decision. People research, compare and wait. The practice that stays in touch, helpfully, is usually the one that wins the first meeting.
