A managed growth service is a done-for-you arrangement where one team sets up and runs the systems that win clients: CRM, AI agents, website, search, reviews and follow-up. Unlike most agencies, it also runs what happens after the click, so every enquiry is answered, followed up and reported through to a signed client.

## What does a managed growth service include?

It includes the full client acquisition system, run monthly. The core parts are a CRM pipeline, AI agents that answer calls and chats, online booking, a website and landing pages, search and answer engine optimisation, review requests and follow-up campaigns. You approve the plan and take the meetings. The provider runs the rest.

The important word is managed. Software alone does not win clients. Someone has to write the follow-up, keep the pipeline clean, add pages, chase reviews and fix what breaks. In a small firm that job usually falls to a partner at 9 PM. A managed service takes it off them.

## How is it different from a marketing agency?

An agency usually generates attention: ads, social posts, sometimes a website. It hands the lead to you. A managed growth service owns the response as well: the reply in minutes, the booking, the reminders, the proposal chase and the review request. The difference shows up in how results are measured.

| | Marketing agency | In-house marketing hire | Managed growth service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main focus | Campaigns and traffic | Whatever the firm prioritises | Enquiry to signed client |
| Answers enquiries | Usually not | Business hours | AI agents, day and night, with handover to your team |
| CRM and follow-up | Rarely included | Depends on skills | Included and run for you |
| Measured by | Clicks, impressions, leads | Varies | Bookings and new clients |
| Cover when someone is away | Agency team | Gaps during leave | Shared team and systems |
| Cost shape | Monthly retainer plus ad spend | Salary, super and tools | Published monthly plan |

## Who is a managed growth service best for?

It suits professional services firms that want more clients but have no marketing team: accountants, bookkeepers, financial planners, mortgage brokers and other finance firms, plus entrepreneurs and start-ups building from scratch. It suits less well a firm that already has a strong in-house marketing team and only needs software.

Ask three questions. Do enquiries go unanswered when you are with clients? Do proposals go quiet without a follow-up? Do you know which source brought last month's new clients? If any answer is no, the gap is in the system, not the advertising.

## What happens in the first 30 days?

The first month is about getting the system live and answering every enquiry. A good provider follows four steps: a strategy call to map where enquiries come from and where they are lost, a set-up of the CRM pipeline, AI agents, booking and review requests around how your firm works, a go-live where every call, form and chat is captured, and a first report on enquiries, bookings and new clients.

Expect to spend a few hours in that month: approving scripts for the AI, confirming your services and prices, and connecting your calendar. After that, your time goes into the meetings the system books, not the system itself. If a provider needs much more of your time than that each month, it is not really managed.

## How do you judge whether it is working?

Judge it by booked meetings and new clients, not activity. A good provider reports, every month, how many enquiries came in, how fast they were answered, how many booked, and how many became clients. If a report only shows posts published and impressions, ask for the conversion numbers.

| Measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| First response time | Slow replies lose enquiries to faster firms |
| Booking rate | Shows whether the AI and follow-up are converting |
| New clients by source | Shows where to spend more |
| Google reviews added | Lifts trust and local search visibility |
