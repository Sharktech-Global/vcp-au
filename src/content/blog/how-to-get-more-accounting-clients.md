The fastest way to get more accounting clients is to win more of the enquiries you already receive. Reply within minutes, book the discovery call on the spot, follow up every proposal until you get a yes or a no, and ask happy clients for referrals and reviews. New marketing channels work far better once those basics are in place.

## Where do new accounting clients come from?

Most new clients come from four places: referrals from clients and other professionals, Google searches for an accountant nearby, directories such as the CPA Australia and CA ANZ listings, and existing clients who need more services. Every one of these sources ends in the same moment: a call, form or email that someone has to answer.

That moment is where firms lose clients. A business owner looking for a new accountant may contact several firms, and a clear, fast reply with a time to talk gives you the best chance.

Demand is not the problem. The Australian Bureau of Statistics counted 460,461 business entries in 2025-26, and 2,814,778 actively trading businesses at 30 June 2026. Every new business needs registrations, bookkeeping, BAS and tax help. The question is whether your firm catches its share.

## Why do accounting firms lose prospective clients?

Firms lose prospective clients through slow replies, missed calls, proposals that nobody follows up, and enquiries that sit in a shared inbox. The prospect simply books with another firm, and your team never knows the opportunity existed.

Common leaks in professional services firms:

- **Missed calls during client meetings.** The caller hangs up and rings the next firm on the list.
- **Website forms answered the next day.** By then the prospect has already booked elsewhere.
- **Proposals sent once.** No reminder, no second email, no call. The deal goes quiet.
- **Tax time overload.** The busiest weeks are also when new enquiries peak, and when they are most likely to be ignored.
- **No record of where clients came from.** You cannot grow a channel you cannot measure.

Fix these leaks before you spend more on advertising.

## How fast should an accounting firm reply to a new enquiry?

Reply within minutes, not hours. If your team is with clients, an AI agent can answer the call or form, ask a few qualifying questions and book a discovery call into your calendar, so the prospect leaves with a time, not a promise.

## Which client acquisition channels work best for accountants?

Referrals and Google search usually bring the best clients at the lowest cost, because the prospect already trusts you or is actively looking. Partnerships with other professionals add steady volume. Paid ads and social media can work, but only when every enquiry they create is answered and followed up quickly.

| Channel | Best for | Cost | What makes it work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client referrals | Clients like your best current clients | Low | Asking at the right moment, and tracking every referral |
| Professional partners (lawyers, brokers, financial planners) | Steady flow of business clients | Low | A clear brief on who you help, and fast replies to their introductions |
| Google Business Profile and local search | Owners searching for an accountant nearby | Low to medium | Complete profile, genuine reviews, a website that books calls |
| Directories (CPA Australia, CA ANZ, software advisor listings) | High intent prospects comparing firms | Low | A complete profile and a reply within minutes |
| Existing clients needing more services | Bookkeeping, payroll and advisory upsell | Very low | A planned review meeting, not a passing mention |
| Paid search and social ads | Fast volume in a new niche | Medium to high | Instant reply and follow-up, or the spend is wasted |

## How do you build a referral routine that works?

Ask for referrals at a moment of value, such as after a clean lodgement, a tax saving or a solved problem. Be specific about the type of client you want. Track every referral, thank the referrer whatever the outcome, and reply to referred prospects the same day. A routine beats an occasional favour.

A simple routine looks like this:

1. **Trigger the ask.** When a job is marked complete, send a short thank you message with a review request and a referral prompt.
2. **Name the ideal client.** "Do you know another café owner who needs help with BAS?" works better than "send people our way".
3. **Track the source.** Record who referred each new prospect, so you know which clients and partners send the best work.
4. **Close the loop.** Tell the referrer when their introduction becomes a client.

If you send marketing emails or SMS messages, follow the Spam Act. The Australian Communications and Media Authority says you need consent, must identify yourself as the sender and must make it easy to unsubscribe.

## Do Google reviews help an accounting firm get clients?

Yes. A prospect comparing firms on Google sees your rating and recent reviews before they ever visit your website. Ask every satisfied client for an honest review after each engagement, and reply to every review. Never write, buy or filter reviews: the ACCC says fake or misleading reviews are against the law.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest way to get more accounting clients?

Win more of the enquiries you already receive. Faster replies, proposal follow-up and a referral routine cost far less than new advertising, and they make every other channel work harder.

### How many times should I follow up a proposal?

Follow up until you get a clear yes or no. A short sequence of email and SMS reminders over two to three weeks, with an easy way to book a call, keeps the proposal in front of the prospect without extra admin.

### Can an AI agent answer tax questions from prospects?

No. The AI captures the enquiry, asks qualifying questions and books a call. Tax and accounting advice stays with your registered team.
