Professional services firms choose one partner for IT and growth because it removes the gaps between suppliers. One team owns the helpdesk, security, CRM, website and AI agents, so problems get fixed instead of passed around, and every enquiry is answered and tracked. The trade-off is that the partner must be good at both, so check before you commit.

## Why does having separate IT and marketing suppliers cost firms clients?

Separate suppliers cost clients because the hand-offs fail. The website agency does not own the phone system, the IT provider does not own the CRM, and nobody owns the moment a prospect calls and nobody answers. Each supplier can be doing its own job well while the firm still loses the enquiry.

Picture a typical week in a small accounting or advice practice. The phones forward to a mobile that is in a client meeting. The website form emails an inbox nobody checks until Friday. The CRM, if there is one, was set up by someone who has since left. When a prospect slips through, every supplier can show that their piece worked.

## What does one partner for IT and growth actually cover?

It covers two layers run by one team. The IT layer keeps the firm running and safe: helpdesk, Microsoft 365, devices, patching, backups and security. The growth layer wins clients: CRM, AI agents on calls and chat, website, search, reviews and follow-up. One partner means one plan, one point of contact and one report.

## Is a combined partner right for every firm?

No. A combined partner suits firms of roughly 5 to 50 staff that have no in-house IT or marketing team and want fewer suppliers. A larger firm with its own IT department may only need the growth layer. A sole practitioner may only need a simple front desk system to start.

## What should you check before you sign?

Check five things: a written scope, published prices, who owns your data and accounts, how the AI is limited, and how results are reported. If a partner cannot answer each clearly, keep looking.

1. **Scope in writing.** What is included each month, and what is quoted separately.
2. **Prices.** Both annual and month to month where they differ. For example, Elevate is $300 + GST a month billed annually, or $360 month to month.
3. **Ownership.** Your domain, Microsoft 365 tenancy and client data stay yours.
4. **AI limits.** The AI should not give financial, credit or tax advice. It should answer general questions and book a call with your licensed or registered team.
5. **Reporting.** Enquiries, bookings and new clients each month, not just activity.

Privacy matters too. The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner recommends that businesses do due diligence on AI products, keep personal information out of public AI tools, and clearly label chatbots as AI ([OAIC guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products)).
