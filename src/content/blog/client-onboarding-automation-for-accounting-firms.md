Client onboarding automation for an accounting firm means every step from first enquiry to first job runs without manual chasing. The enquiry gets a reply in minutes, the discovery call books itself, the engagement letter and documents are requested and reminded, and your team only steps in to review, decide and do the work.

## What does client onboarding include for an accounting firm?

Onboarding covers everything between "I need an accountant" and the first job in your practice software. That includes the first reply, the discovery call, the proposal, the engagement letter, identity and due diligence checks, ATO agent linking, document collection and the welcome. Most firms only automate the second half, so the first half leaks clients.

Think of onboarding as two stages.

**Stage one: winning the client.** Reply, qualify, book the call and follow up the proposal until it is signed.

**Stage two: setting the client up.** Engagement letter, identity checks, due diligence, agent linking, documents and the first job.

Most onboarding tools focus on stage two. That matters, but a client lost in stage one never reaches it.

## Why did onboarding get harder for Australian firms in 2026?

Two changes added steps. From 1 July 2026, accountants who provide certain designated services came under the anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing regime, which includes customer due diligence and record keeping. The ATO is also extending client-to-agent linking to individuals and sole traders, adding a client confirmation step.

AUSTRAC says the number of businesses it regulates grows from around 19,000 to close to 100,000 nationwide with the new sectors. Obligations include an AML/CTF program, customer due diligence, suspicious matter reporting and record keeping.

The ATO has published a timeline for individuals and sole traders: a pilot from March 2027, then full deployment in November 2027. After that, client-to-agent linking becomes the only way for registered agents to add new individual and sole trader clients. The agent starts the request, and the client confirms it in the ATO app or through myGov.

Every extra step is another place a new client can stall. Automation keeps each step moving with reminders, so your team is not chasing by hand.

A note on compliance. This article is not legal or tax advice. Whether your services are designated services, and what your program needs, is a question for your firm and AUSTRAC's guidance.

## Which onboarding steps should you automate first?

Start with the steps that cost you clients, then the steps that cost you hours. For most firms that means first response and booking first, then proposal follow-up, then document and detail requests. Compliance checks and practice software set-up come next, using tools built for that job.

Here is a practical order:

1. **First reply.** Every call, form and chat gets an answer in minutes, day or night.
2. **Discovery call booking.** The prospect picks a time in your calendar. Reminders cut no-shows.
3. **Proposal follow-up.** Email and SMS reminders until the client signs or says no.
4. **Detail and document requests.** Business details, prior returns and software access, chased automatically.
5. **Identity and due diligence.** Use a tool built for checks and record keeping.
6. **Agent linking instructions.** A plain-English message telling the client exactly what to confirm.
7. **Welcome and first review request.** A welcome message, then a review request once the first job is done.

## How do the main onboarding approaches compare?

Firms usually combine tools. Email and spreadsheets cost nothing but rely on memory. Practice software runs the work. Dedicated onboarding apps handle letters and checks. A CRM with an AI agent wins the client first. The right mix depends on which gap costs you most today.

| Approach | Covers | Strength | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email, PDF forms and spreadsheets | Everything, by hand | Free and familiar | Slow, easy to forget, nothing reminds the client |
| Practice management software | Jobs, deadlines and client records | Runs the client work | Little help replying to new enquiries or chasing proposals |
| Dedicated onboarding and compliance apps | Engagement letters, identity checks, document collection | Strong on stage two | Starts after the client has already chosen you |
| VCPility | First reply, booking, pipeline, follow-up and reviews | Wins the client and keeps them moving, set up for you | Not a practice management or compliance system, by design |

For many firms, the right set-up is a front end that wins the client and a back end that sets them up. Connect the two and nobody retypes details.

## How fast should an accounting firm reply to a new enquiry?

Reply within minutes. A business owner looking for an accountant often contacts several firms at once and books with the first clear answer. If your team is with clients, an AI agent can reply, ask qualifying questions and book a discovery call. Onboarding speed starts with that first reply, not the engagement letter.

Demand is steady. The Australian Bureau of Statistics counted 460,461 business entries in 2025-26 and 2,814,778 actively trading businesses at 30 June 2026. Each new business needs registrations, bookkeeping, BAS and tax help.

Good onboarding feels personal to the client and invisible to your team. The client gets fast replies, clear next steps and reminders at the right time. Your team sees every prospect in one pipeline and only touches the file to review, decide or advise. Nothing goes quiet between steps.

It is software that runs the repeat steps of bringing on a new client: replies, bookings, reminders, document requests and welcome messages. Your team reviews and advises, and the system handles the chasing.

No. Practice management software runs jobs and deadlines. Onboarding automation wins the client and collects what you need, then hands over to your practice tools.
