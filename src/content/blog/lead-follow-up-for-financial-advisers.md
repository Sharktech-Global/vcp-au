Good lead follow-up for financial advisers means replying to every enquiry within minutes, booking a first meeting while interest is high, and staying in touch with helpful, approved messages until the person is ready. It must also be respectful. No pressure, no cold calls, and only messages people have agreed to receive.

That second part matters more in 2026 than ever.

## Why does lead follow-up matter so much for advice practices?

Most people who enquire about financial advice are not ready to sign that day. They are comparing, asking their partner, or waiting for a life event. A practice that replies fast and keeps in touch, helpfully, is usually the one that gets the first meeting. A practice that replies once and waits usually loses the prospect to silence.

The problem is rarely the number of enquiries. It is what happens after them. Typical gaps look like this:

- A website form arrives at 8 PM and nobody sees it until the next afternoon.
- A prospect asks a question on Facebook and the reply goes to one staff member's inbox.
- Someone books a first meeting, gets no reminder, and does not turn up.
- A prospect says "not yet" and never hears from the practice again.

Each one is a lost client you already paid to attract.

## What is ASIC concerned about with lead generation?

ASIC is reviewing advice licensees that use lead generation services, because some practices pressure people into switching their superannuation. Warning signs it lists include pressure to act immediately, free "super health checks", offers to find lost super, unlicensed people in the process and mostly phone contact with little time with an adviser.

ASIC started this review in February 2026 and publishes a list of lead generators, referral partners and advice businesses that acquired leads. It warns that licensed businesses that engage lead generators acting this way share the risk. The [ASIC media release of 18 February 2026](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-029mr-asic-commences-new-review-of-advice-licensees-that-use-lead-generation-services/) sets this out, and Moneysmart updated its consumer page on [lead generation and how it works](https://moneysmart.gov.au/investment-warnings/lead-generation-and-how-it-works) on 9 November 2026.

Being named is not a finding that the law was broken. But how you find and follow up prospects now matters.

## What does compliant lead follow-up look like?

Compliant follow-up starts with people who asked to hear from you. Reply quickly to their enquiry, identify your practice clearly, give them an easy way to opt out, and never push them to act today. Book them with a licensed adviser rather than letting anyone else discuss their money.

The Spam Act sets the basic rules for emails and SMS. The [ACMA guidance on avoiding spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) says you need consent before sending marketing messages, every message must identify you and include your contact details, and it must be easy to unsubscribe. You also need to keep a record of when and how consent was given.

Here is how the two approaches compare:

| Follow-up practice | Respectful, compliant follow-up | High-risk follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Where leads come from | Your own website, referrals, events and social pages | Bought lists and "free health check" ads |
| First contact | Fast reply to an enquiry the person made | Unsolicited calls to people who never asked |
| Tone | Helpful, no deadline pressure | "Act now" urgency |
| Who discusses their money | A licensed adviser in a meeting | Call centre staff or unlicensed people |
| Messages after that | Approved emails and SMS with an unsubscribe link | Repeated calls with no clear opt out |
| Record keeping | Consent and every contact logged in a CRM | Scattered across phones and inboxes |

## How fast should an adviser reply to a new enquiry?

Within minutes, during and after business hours. A fast first reply simply confirms you received the enquiry, says who will help, and offers a link to book a first meeting. An automated, personal message you have approved does the job.

## What should a follow-up sequence for prospects include?

A simple sequence for someone who enquired but has not booked could look like this:

1. **Minute one.** An automatic email and SMS: thanks, who you are, a booking link.
2. **Same day.** A short call or personal message from your team, if the person gave a phone number for that purpose.
3. **Day three.** A helpful note answering a common question, such as what happens in a first meeting.
4. **Day seven.** A reminder that the booking link is still open, with no deadline.
5. **Monthly after that.** Practice news or general education your licensee has approved, until they book or unsubscribe.

For people who do book, send a confirmation, a reminder the day before and a note on what to bring.

Keep every message general. Anything about a person's own super, investments, insurance or strategy belongs in a meeting with a licensed adviser. Your licensee and compliance team should approve the templates before they go live.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use automated emails and SMS to follow up financial advice leads?

Yes, if the person consented to hear from you. Each message must identify your practice, include contact details and an easy unsubscribe, as the ACMA guidance on spam sets out. Keep the content general and have your licensee approve the templates.

### Should advice practices buy leads?

That is a decision for your practice and your licensee. ASIC is reviewing advice licensees that use lead generation services and warns that licensees share the risk when lead generators use pressure or mislead people. Leads that come from your own website and referrals are easier to follow up with confidence.

### How many times should I follow up a prospect?

Keep going while you are helpful and they have not unsubscribed. A common pattern is a reply in minutes, a personal contact the same day, two more messages in the first week, then a monthly newsletter. Stop straight away if they ask you to.

### Can an AI chatbot answer questions about someone's super?

No. It should answer general questions about your practice, such as what a first meeting covers and how fees are explained, and book the person with a licensed adviser for anything about their own situation.
