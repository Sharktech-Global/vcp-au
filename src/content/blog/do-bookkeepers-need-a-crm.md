Yes, most Australian bookkeepers need a CRM once new enquiries arrive faster than they can reply. Your accounting software tracks client books, not prospects. A CRM catches every call, form and message, books the discovery call, chases onboarding details and asks for reviews, so new clients stop slipping away while you work.

## Why is accounting software not enough to grow a practice?

Accounting and practice software is built to run client work: ledgers, BAS preparation, payroll and deadlines. It is not built to answer a new enquiry at 7 pm, book a first call or follow up a proposal. Growth happens before a client is ever set up in your ledger, and that stage needs its own system.

Most bookkeepers live inside their ledger platform all day. That is the right place for the work.

The gap sits earlier. A business owner finds you on Google. They call. You are mid-reconciliation, so the call rings out. They try the next bookkeeper on the list.

No ledger system records that lost client. A CRM does.

## How do you know your practice needs a CRM?

You need a CRM when enquiries depend on your memory or your inbox. Common signs are missed calls during busy weeks, web forms answered days later, proposals nobody chases, onboarding documents requested by email three times, and no clear view of which referral partners send work. Two or more of these signs mean revenue is leaking.

Run this quick check on your last month:

- **Did any enquiry wait more than a day for a reply?**
- **Did a prospect go quiet after you sent a proposal?**
- **Did onboarding stall while you chased access or documents?**
- **Can you name your three best referral sources with numbers?**
- **Did you ask every happy client for a Google review?**

If you answered "yes" to the first three, or "no" to the last two, the problem is follow-up, not bookkeeping skill.

## Is there enough demand to justify the setup?

Yes. Demand for bookkeeping keeps growing because new businesses keep starting. The Australian Bureau of Statistics counted 460,461 business entries in 2025-26 and 2,814,778 actively trading businesses at 30 June 2026. Every new business needs its books, BAS and payroll handled, and the fastest reply often wins the work.

That growth is spread across every state. New South Wales recorded the largest net increase, with 26,057 more businesses in the year.

More businesses means more enquiries. It also means more bookkeepers competing for the same searches. The practice that replies first, books the call and follows up cleanly is the one that grows.

## What should a CRM for bookkeepers actually do?

A good bookkeeper CRM handles the full path from first enquiry to signed client. It should reply to every call and message within minutes, book discovery calls into your calendar, track each prospect in a simple pipeline, automate onboarding requests, ask for reviews after each engagement, and report where new clients came from.

Use this checklist when you compare options:

1. **Fast first response.** An AI agent that replies to calls, chats and forms in minutes, day or night.
2. **Online booking.** Prospects choose a time without email tag. Reminders go out automatically.
3. **A clear pipeline.** Enquiry, discovery call, proposal, engagement, onboarding. You see every stage at a glance.
4. **Onboarding sequences.** Automated email and SMS that collect business details, software access and documents.
5. **Review requests.** Sent after each engagement, so you rank higher in local search.
6. **Source reporting.** Google, referrals, social and ads, side by side.
7. **Works beside your ledger platform.** It should not replace your bookkeeping software.
8. **Published Australian pricing.** No long sales process for a small practice.

A note on compliance. The Tax Practitioners Board states that you must be registered with it to provide BAS services for a fee. A CRM does not change that. It should support your process, and any BAS or tax question from a prospect should go to you or your registered team, not to an automated reply.

## How do the main options compare?

Bookkeepers usually choose between four types of tool. Each does a different job. The right choice depends on whether your bottleneck is running client work or winning new clients.

| Option | Built for | Strength | Gap for winning new clients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheets and email | Getting started | Free and familiar | Nothing replies, reminds or reports for you |
| Practice management platforms | Running client work | Jobs, deadlines, workflows | Little or no enquiry handling or first response |
| General CRMs (large global brands) | Sales teams | Powerful pipelines | Setup and training suit larger teams, not a sole practice |
| VCPility | Winning and onboarding new clients | AI agent replies, books, follows up and asks for reviews, set up for you | Not a bookkeeping or practice management system, by design |

Many practices end up with two systems that work side by side. One runs the books. One wins the clients.

## How fast should a bookkeeper reply to a new enquiry?

Reply within minutes, not hours. A business owner looking for a bookkeeper often contacts several practices at once and books with the first one that responds clearly. If you cannot reply yourself during client work, an AI agent can answer, capture the details and book a discovery call into your calendar.

Speed matters most in peak periods: around BAS lodgement dates, the end of financial year and when new businesses register. These are exactly the weeks you have the least spare time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do sole bookkeepers need a CRM?

Often, yes. A sole bookkeeper has no receptionist, so every enquiry that arrives during client work waits. A simple CRM with an AI agent replies, books the call and keeps the prospect warm until you are free.

### Does a CRM replace my bookkeeping software?

No. Your ledger and practice software keep running the client work. A CRM handles enquiries, follow-up, onboarding and reviews, and sits beside the tools you already use.

### Can a CRM help with client onboarding?

Yes. Automated email and SMS sequences can request business details, software access and documents, and remind the client until everything arrives. That saves unbillable hours of chasing.

### Can the AI answer tax or BAS questions?

No. The AI captures the enquiry and books a call. Advice on tax and BAS matters stays with you or your registered team, in line with your Tax Practitioners Board obligations.
