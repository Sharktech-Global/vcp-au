Moving CRM without losing data is a records job. This CRM migration checklist is for owners and managers of Australian service businesses, including accountants, bookkeepers, financial planners, mortgage brokers and growing small businesses, who are leaving a spreadsheet or an old CRM. If you switch CRM in a small Australian business, do the work in this order: export, clean, map, import, then check.

**Key facts**

- Export contacts and history to CSV, or the provider's spreadsheet export, before you cancel.
- Read the contract for the notice period, the last day you can log in, and what happens to the data when the subscription ends.
- The OAIC states that a small business has annual turnover of $3 million or less, and that most small businesses are not covered by the Privacy Act. If the Act covers you, the Australian Privacy Principles apply to the client information you move.
- The Spam Act 2003 requires consent for commercial email and SMS. Record who consented, when and how, and keep the unsubscribe list.
- Store the export in an account your business controls. Tidy a copy, and leave the original file unchanged.

This is general information, not legal advice.

## What should you do before you switch CRM?

Before you switch CRM, export a full copy of your data, read the notice period in your contract, and name who will check the import. List the fields your team uses each week, such as name, phone, email, source and next step. Keep the old login until the new records have been checked against that export.

Read the date access ends, and any fee for a later export. Give notice when you can still download the file on the last day. Hold that file in an account your business controls. ## What data should you export?

Export every record you would struggle to rebuild: contacts, notes, pipeline stage, open tasks, appointments, tags, file links and consent history. Ask the provider for a full account export if the on-screen download is contacts with no history. Save the file to an account your business controls, then work from a copy.

Ask for CSV and open the file before you log out. Pull name, phone, email, address, company, owner, history, open tasks, and consent (when, how, and who unsubscribed). Date a spreadsheet copy, then clean another.

## How do you clean and map fields?

Clean a copy of the export, not the original. Merge duplicate people, fix obvious errors in names and emails, and drop rows you know are test records. Then write a field map: each old column name beside the field it will fill in the new CRM. Import a small batch and read those records before you load the rest.

Match duplicates on email, or on phone where the email is blank. Keep one row per person and fold spare notes into it. Map full name to the contact name, mobile to phone (keep the Australian dialling code), status to the pipeline stage, and unsubscribe to the email consent flag. If a note, phone number or opt-out is missing in the test batch, fix the map before you load the rest.

## How do you keep email and SMS consent records?

Bring the consent record across with the contact. For each person, keep who consented, when, how (a form, a phone call or in person) and whether it covered email, SMS or both. The Spam Act 2003 puts the burden on you to show consent. Also bring the unsubscribe list, so people who opted out are not messaged again.

The [ACMA guidance on avoiding spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) says you need consent before a commercial email or SMS, you must identify your business, and you must offer an unsubscribe. Load the unsubscribe list before anyone sends, and keep email and SMS as separate flags. Skip a bought list unless each address has its own consent record, and skip any list built with address-harvesting software. Where the file does not show how consent was given, ask the person or leave them off marketing. The ACMA states that an unsubscribe must be honoured within 5 working days.

## How long does a CRM migration take?

There is no single duration. It depends on how many records you have, how messy they are, how many fields you map, and the notice period in your current contract. Plan time for cleaning and checking as well as the import. Keep the old system running until the counts and a sample of records match.

Use the notice period as your overlap, and confirm you can still export on the last day of access. Fuller VCPility set-ups, including contact imports, are scheduled during onboarding. ## What should you check after go-live?

After go-live, compare the number of records exported with the number imported, then open a sample of contacts and read the notes, owner, stage and consent flag. Send a test email and SMS to yourself. Watch the next few real enquiries land in the new CRM. Leave the old login in place until that check is done.

Include a long-standing client, an open prospect, someone who unsubscribed, and a row that had a note. A form should create a contact, a booking should hit the right calendar, and an opt-out should still be an opt-out. Pause campaigns if the sample is wrong. ## What should your CRM migration checklist include?

Work through this list from top to bottom. Save the export and write the field map before the full import, and read a sample of records before you cancel. Keep the list beside the dated export so anyone on the team can see what has been done.

1. Read the contract: notice, last login, export rights, deletion.
2. Export contacts, notes, tasks, files and consent to CSV.
3. Store the file in an account your business controls.
4. Clean a copy. Keep the original as downloaded.
5. Merge duplicates. Fix names, emails and phones.
6. Map fields, including consent and unsubscribe.
7. Import a small batch and compare it with the file.
8. Import the rest and compare the row counts.
9. Spot-check notes, owners, stages and opt-outs.
10. Test a form, a booking, an email and an SMS.
11. Tell the team the cut-over date.
12. Cancel after the check, inside the notice rules.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I cancel the old CRM before the export is saved?

No. Download the file and open it while you can still log in. Read the contract for how long data is kept after the account ends, and for any retrieval fee, before you give notice.

### Do the Australian Privacy Principles apply when I move client data?

They apply if the Privacy Act covers your business. The OAIC says most businesses with turnover of $3 million or less are not covered, with exceptions such as health service providers, businesses that trade in personal information, and anti-money laundering reporting entities. See the [OAIC small business guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-for-organisations/small-business). This is general information, not legal advice.

### What if our contacts are in a spreadsheet?

Save a dated copy, clean a second copy, and map each column to a field. Consent and unsubscribe checks still apply if you email or text those people. Pipeline stages and getting the team using the new record are covered in [moving from a spreadsheet to a CRM](/blog/spreadsheet-to-crm).

### How do I avoid emailing people who have unsubscribed?

Load the unsubscribe list before you send from the new CRM. New software does not create fresh consent. The [ACMA spam guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) says you must show consent and honour an unsubscribe request.
