Most Australian start-ups begin the same way.

A free CRM. A spreadsheet. A shared inbox. A booking link. A form tool. A few automations stitched together on a weekend.

It works until the first real growth. Then leads slip between the gaps, and nobody owns the follow-up.

## What founders actually need from a CRM

Founders do not need the biggest CRM. They need one that does the work they have no time for:

- **Capture every lead** from the website, social, events and referrals.
- **Reply fast**, even while you are pitching or building.
- **Follow up automatically** until the prospect says yes or no.
- **Book meetings** without email tennis.
- **Collect reviews** from the first customers.
- **Show the numbers**: leads, conversion and pipeline you can put in front of investors.

## The common traps

- **Free tiers that stop at the wrong moment.** The features you need for growth sit behind expensive upgrades.
- **Enterprise CRMs too early.** Powerful, but they need an administrator you have not hired.
- **Too many tools.** Every new tool adds a login, a bill and another place for leads to hide.
- **Do-it-yourself setup.** Weeks lost configuring pipelines instead of selling.

## How the options compare

| Option | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Free CRM tiers | Getting started | Limits on automation and contacts as you grow |
| Large global CRMs | Funded sales teams | Setup time, administration and per-seat cost |
| Stack of point tools | Flexibility | Leads lost between tools, many bills |
