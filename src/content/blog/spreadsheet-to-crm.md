A spreadsheet is a fair place for a short customer list: a name, a phone number, a note. After hours, a lead you meant to call is still there, a second copy says something else, and the person who updates the sheet is away.

If you are still choosing between the two tools, read [CRM vs spreadsheets for Australian small businesses](/blog/crm-vs-spreadsheets-australia). The export order is the [CRM migration checklist](/blog/crm-migration-checklist). What to look for in the system is in [what a small business CRM should include](/blog/best-crm-for-small-business-australia).

**Key facts**

- A spreadsheet stores what someone types. It does not send a reminder, and it does not show who followed up unless that was written down.
- Two copies of the same file are two versions of the customer list. A file on one laptop is unavailable when that person is away.
- The [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business) says a small business is one with an annual turnover of $3 million or less, and that most small businesses are not covered by the Privacy Act 1988. Some are. The page lists those cases.
- If the Privacy Act covers you, the Australian Privacy Principles apply. [APP 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information) requires reasonable steps to protect personal information you hold.
- The [Spam Act 2003](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) requires consent before a marketing email or SMS. Putting the list into a CRM does not create that consent.

This is general information, not legal advice.

## What are the signs a spreadsheet has stopped working?

The spreadsheet has stopped working when a lead slips through, two people work from different copies, nobody can see who followed up, and the file sits on one laptop. A date in a column does not send itself. Someone has to open the file and act.

If that is rare, keep the sheet. If it happens most weeks, move the live work. The comparison is in [CRM vs spreadsheets](/blog/crm-vs-spreadsheets-australia).

## What should you move from the spreadsheet before the rest?

Move the people you still expect to contact, and the next step for each of them, before the old history. Take name, phone, email, source, owner, stage and the date someone should act. Rows you will not contact can stay in an archive copy. The team needs a working list on Monday.

The [CRM migration checklist](/blog/crm-migration-checklist) is the wider order: export, clean, map, import, then check. Tidy a copy and leave the original as you saved it. If a supplier holds the file, [who owns your data](/blog/who-owns-your-website-domain-and-data) covers the right to export.

## How do you clean and de-duplicate contacts before you import?

Clean a copy, not the original. Merge people who appear twice, fix obvious errors in names and emails, and drop test rows. Match duplicates on email. Where the email is blank, match on phone, and keep the Australian dialling code.

Keep one row per person and fold spare notes into it. If two rows might be different people, leave them and mark them for a check. Bring consent and the unsubscribe list with the contact. The [CRM migration checklist](/blog/crm-migration-checklist) sets that out.

## How do you map spreadsheet columns to CRM fields?

Write the map before you import: each column name beside the CRM field it will fill. Import a small batch, open those records, and fix the map before you load the rest. That stops a notes column landing in the phone field.

- Name column to contact name. If given name and surname share a cell, split them on the copy and check a hyphenated name.
- Mobile to phone, in one form for the whole file: leading zero, or +61.
- Email left as email. Leave blanks blank.
- Status to pipeline stage. Note what each old value means.
- Next date to a follow-up task. A past date becomes a task for this week.
- Consent and unsubscribe to separate email and SMS flags, with who agreed, when and how.

If a note, a number or an opt-out is missing in the test batch, fix the map before the full load. The [done-for-you CRM set-up](/web-services/crm-setup) imports spreadsheets and most CRMs during onboarding. You still supply the file and read the result.

## How do you set pipeline stages and follow-up reminders?

Set stages that match how a prospect becomes a client, and give each open contact one next action. A short set is enough for a small firm: new enquiry, contacted, appointment booked, proposal sent, won. Add a stage when the team already treats that moment as different work.

The [features page](/features#crm) lists contact management, pipeline tracking, deal insights, and follow-ups that schedule reminders, emails and SMS. You pick the send time. For a marketing message, the [ACMA rules](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) are consent, identification and an unsubscribe. Load opt-outs before anyone sends. A no-show still uses a gap you had held.

## How do you get staff to use the CRM?

Staff use a CRM when the morning question has one answer: who needs a follow-up today, written on the contact. If that answer still sits in a private inbox or a second spreadsheet, the new system will be quiet by the end of the week.

Enter enquiries the day they arrive, including calls taken with a client in the room. Write the note and the next date before you leave the record. After cut-over, new work goes into the CRM. ## How do you keep customer data secure when you move it?

Treat the export as a customer list. Store it in an account the business controls, limit who can download it, and do not leave the single copy on one laptop or in a personal inbox. Forwarding the file so someone can update a row is how spare copies start, often during that late admin session.

The [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business) says a small business has annual turnover of $3 million or less, and that most are not covered by the Privacy Act 1988. Some are covered whatever the turnover, including health service providers and businesses that trade in personal information. If the Act covers you, the Australian Privacy Principles apply. [APP 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information) requires reasonable steps against misuse, interference, loss, and unauthorised access, modification or disclosure. The OAIC also recommends protecting personal information where the Act does not apply.

Save it to a business account, share it with the people doing the import, and close that share after the check. Keep consent on the contact. Delete spare copies once the records match.

The [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) says you need consent before a marketing email or SMS, and you must be able to show it. A form, a ticked box, a phone call or a conversation in person can be express consent. Record who, when and how. Honour an unsubscribe within 5 working days, with no fee and no login. Do not use a list built with address-harvesting software. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know the spreadsheet has stopped working?

Leads slip, copies disagree, follow-up depends on memory, or the list lives on one laptop. If that is rare, keep the sheet. See [CRM vs spreadsheets](/blog/crm-vs-spreadsheets-australia).

### What should I move before the rest of the file?

People you still expect to contact, with a stage, an owner and a next date. Leave the rest in an archive copy. The full export order is the [CRM migration checklist](/blog/crm-migration-checklist).

### How do I stop duplicate contacts landing in the CRM?

Clean a copy before you import. Match on email, or on phone where the email is blank, and keep one row per person. Read a small batch before you load the rest.

### Can I email the whole list once it is in the CRM?

Message someone when you can show consent for that commercial email or SMS. A new CRM does not create consent. The [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) requires consent, identification, and an unsubscribe honoured within 5 working days.

### Does the Privacy Act cover a small business that moves its customer list?

The OAIC says a small business has annual turnover of $3 million or less, and that most are not covered, with exceptions such as health service providers and businesses that trade in personal information. If the Act covers you, the Australian Privacy Principles apply, including APP 11. See the [OAIC small business page](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business). This is general information, not legal advice.
