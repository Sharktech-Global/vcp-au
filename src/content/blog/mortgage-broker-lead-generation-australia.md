Mortgage broker lead generation in Australia works best when you own your lead sources and fix your conversion. The strongest leads come from referral partners, past clients, Google search and your reviews. Bought leads can fill a gap, but they are often shared. Whatever the source, the broker who replies first and follows up longest usually wins.

Most advice on this topic stops at "get more leads". This article covers both halves: where the leads come from, and what happens in the first hour after they arrive.

## Why is lead generation harder for brokers now?

Brokers now settle most new home loans in Australia, so borrowers have plenty of brokers to choose from. Cotality data commissioned by the MFAA shows brokers settled 81.6% of new residential home loans in the June 2026 quarter. More brokers chasing the same borrower means speed and follow-up decide who wins.

That figure was reported by [Australian Broker on 3 September 2026](https://www.brokernews.com.au/news/breaking-news/mortgage-brokers-keep-winning-market-share-289908.aspx). It is good news for the channel. It also means the borrower who fills in your form has probably filled in two others.

## Where do mortgage brokers get their leads?

Most broker leads come from five places: referral partners such as real estate agents and accountants, past clients who refinance or buy again, Google search and your Google Business Profile, social media, and paid or bought leads. The first four build an asset you own. Bought leads stop the day you stop paying.

Here is how the main sources compare.

| Lead source | Cost pattern | Typical intent | Who owns it | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral partners | Time and relationship upkeep | High, warm introduction | You | Partners go quiet without updates |
| Past clients | Low, already in your book | High when timed well | You | Forgotten after settlement |
| Google search and reviews | Content and review effort, builds over time | High, actively searching | You | Slow start, needs consistency |
| Social media | Time, or ad spend | Mixed, often early stage | You | Activity without enquiries |
| Bought leads | Pay per lead or subscription | Varies, often shared | The seller | Stops when payment stops |

No source works on its own. A referral still checks your Google reviews. A search visitor still needs a fast reply.

## Should you buy mortgage leads or build your own?

Bought leads can help a new broker fill the calendar, but they rarely build a business. Many are sold to more than one broker, so you start in a race. Leads you generate yourself, through referrals, past clients and search, cost less over time and keep coming after a campaign ends.

If you do buy leads, treat them like any other enquiry. Reply in minutes, ask the same qualifying questions and track how many settle. Then compare the cost per settled loan, not the cost per lead.

## Why do brokers lose leads they already have?

Most lost leads are not bad leads. They are slow replies, enquiries spread across too many inboxes, and borrowers who were not ready yet and never heard from you again. A borrower researching at 9 pm who hears back the next afternoon has often booked with someone else.

The common leaks:

- **After-hours enquiries** answered the next day.
- **Leads in too many places**: website, Facebook, Instagram, partner emails and phone messages.
- **No follow-up** for borrowers who are six or twelve months away.
- **Missed calls** while you are in a client meeting.

## How do you get more leads from referral partners?

Make referring easy and keep partners informed. Give each real estate agent, accountant or financial planner a simple referral form or link, confirm every referral the same day, and send a short update when the client books, applies and settles. Partners refer more to brokers who make them look good to their own clients.

A simple referral routine:

1. A dedicated referral form on your website, tagged by partner.
2. An automatic thank you to the partner when a referral arrives.
3. A status update at each stage you agree to share, with the client's consent.
4. A regular check-in with every active partner, even when things are quiet.

## How do you turn past clients into new loans?

Your settled clients are your cheapest source of new business. Keep in touch after settlement, invite them to a loan review at sensible intervals, and ask for a Google review while the experience is fresh. Clients who hear from you regularly are more likely to come back and to refer friends.

Keep these messages general. A reminder to book a review with your broker is helpful. Product or rate recommendations belong in a conversation with your licensed team, not in an automated message.

## What rules apply to SMS and email follow-up?

The Spam Act applies to marketing emails and SMS. The Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) sums it up as three rules: you need the person's consent, you must identify who is sending, and every message needs a way to unsubscribe. Build these into every automated follow-up from day one.

The [ACMA's guidance on dealing with spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/dealing-with-spam) sets out what recipients can expect. Record where and when each person gave consent, and honour unsubscribe requests promptly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best lead source for a mortgage broker?

For most brokers, referral partners and past clients produce the warmest leads at the lowest cost. Google search and reviews add a steady flow over time. The best mix depends on your market, so track settled loans by source.

### Are bought mortgage leads worth it?

They can fill gaps, especially for a new broker. Many are shared with other brokers, so reply within minutes and judge them on cost per settled loan, not cost per lead.

### How fast should a broker reply to a new enquiry?

Within minutes, including after hours. Borrowers often contact several brokers at once, and the first useful reply usually gets the conversation.
